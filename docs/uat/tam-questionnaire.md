# Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) questionnaire

Adapted from Davis (1989)'s Perceived Usefulness / Perceived Ease of Use constructs to DoSmart specifically. Administer alongside the SUS questionnaire, after the 4-week independent-use period.

For each statement, mark one: **Strongly disagree (1) — Disagree (2) — Neutral (3) — Agree (4) — Strongly agree (5)**

## Perceived usefulness

1. Using DoSmart improves my ability to keep track of my academic and personal tasks.
2. Using DoSmart helps me get my most important tasks done on time.
3. DoSmart's automatic prioritization reflects what actually matters most to me.
4. The reminders DoSmart sends arrive at times that are actually useful to me.
5. Using DoSmart makes it easier to decide what to work on next.
6. Overall, I find DoSmart useful for managing my workload.

## Perceived ease of use

7. Learning to use DoSmart was easy for me.
8. I find it easy to get DoSmart to do what I want it to do.
9. My interaction with DoSmart is clear and understandable.
10. I find DoSmart flexible to interact with.
11. It was easy for me to become skillful at using DoSmart.
12. Overall, I find DoSmart easy to use.

## Behavioral intention to use

13. I intend to keep using DoSmart after this study ends.
14. I would recommend DoSmart to another student.
15. Given the choice, I would use DoSmart instead of my current method for tracking tasks (paper, another app, memory, etc.).

## Scoring

Report the mean of items 1-6 (Perceived Usefulness), mean of items 7-12 (Perceived Ease of Use), and mean of items 13-15 (Behavioral Intention), each on the 1-5 scale, alongside the SUS score. TAM predicts usefulness and ease of use both drive intention to use — if intention scores are low despite high SUS/usability scores, that's worth digging into in the open-ended debrief rather than assuming the numbers alone tell the whole story.
